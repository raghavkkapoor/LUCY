Siri on steroids.

Play a song, find a file, answer an email, chart a month of data: Lucy runs it on your machine instead of writing about it.

The premise

Chat is the slowest way to use a computer.

Every other assistant answers you. The words have to leave your machine, wait in a queue, get written one token at a time, and come back — and at the end of all that, you still have to go do the thing yourself.

Lucy skips four of those five steps for anything she already knows how to do. The request never leaves the room.

The path

What happens between saying it and it being done.

Five stages, in this order, every time. Nothing here waits on a model.

01

Heard

Your words, the way you said them. No syntax to learn, no prompt to engineer, no keywords to remember. "play my phonk playlist" is a valid command.

> play my phonk playlist
02

Matched

Lucy searches the library for every command that could serve the request and scores them against what you actually asked — not what a keyword lookup thinks you asked. Weak matches are cheap to discard. This is the part that has to be good.

media.resolve 0.94 · media.play 0.91 · media.queue 0.66
03

Ordered

One command almost never finishes a job. Lucy sorts the matches into an order that makes sense on a real system: find before filter, filter before act, act before report.

resolve → play → confirm
04

Chained

The ordered commands become one run. Output from each step feeds the next, and every step knows how to fail without taking the rest of the chain down with it.

lucy chain --steps 3 --halt-on-error --keep-state
05

Run

On your machine. Your files, your sessions, your permissions, your speaker. Then Lucy tells you what she did — out loud, if you want it out loud.

✓ 3 steps · 240ms · phonk (47 tracks) playing

The toolkit

Four things she does without asking anyone.

Mail · SMTP + IMAP

Send it.

Gmail through its old interface, on purpose. It's faster, it doesn't fight automation, and it doesn't depend on an API key you have to keep alive. Read a thread, find the one about the invoice, reply, done.

Media · YouTube

Play it.

If it exists, it's on YouTube — which makes it the whole music library and the whole video library at once. Play anything, run your playlists, pull the top comments on what's in front of you.

Graphics · Raylib

Draw it.

Some answers are a shape, not a sentence. Lucy renders 2D and 3D directly, so a month of numbers arrives as a chart on screen instead of a paragraph describing one.

Voice · Windows TTS

Say it.

Everything comes back out loud. Fixed responses are instant because they're already written; anything a model wrote gets spoken the moment the first line lands, not after the last one.

When it breaks

Most assistants apologize. Lucy hands you the trace.

Something will fail. A path moves, a password expires, a site changes its markup. What matters is what you get back — and "sorry, I ran into an issue" is not an answer, it's a shrug.

You get the command that failed, the step it failed on, the line, the error the system actually returned, and what your machine looked like on both sides of the break. Enough to fix it in one move.

Retry resumes at step 3. The two steps that worked don't run again, and your draft is exactly where you left it. Nothing was half-sent.

The quiet part

She calls a model when a model is the only thing that will do.

Writing something in your voice. Making sense of a request she's never seen. Those are worth the round trip. Playing a song isn't. Finding a file isn't. Checking the weather isn't.

Search

Formatted results, not paragraphs about results. You get the links and the number you were after in less time than it takes a model to write its first sentence.

Cost

Most days Lucy never calls out at all. On the days she does, a free tier covers it. Nobody should need a subscription to open a file.

Consent

Every model call is visible before it happens. You always know the moment something leaves your machine, and you can always say no.
